# Public registration

`POST /w/:slug/registrations` accepts only name, email, the choice `participation` (`spectator`, default: watch, chat, raise a hand; `participant`: wants to speak on camera and microphone) and the optional `marketing_consent` for a published webinar (no registration checkbox since privacy policy v1.6). A browser removed from this webinar (`RoomRemoval`) gets 403 and nothing is created. Duplicate emails preserve identity and receive the same response. Ended webinars reject new registrations. Pending duplicates resend the same confirmation link, allowing recovery after an enqueue failure, at most once per 10 minutes per registration, 6 times per 24 hours per registration and 10 resends per 24 hours per address across all registrations (`RecipientMailThrottle`; the response is identical whether a mail leaves or not). A mail that proves the mailbox is never prevented: the first confirmation of a newly created registration is outside the 10-resend total, the confirmation that accompanies an instant access draws on a separate per-registration security budget (no per-address total; when it is refused the instant access is refused, never granted without the mail), and the speaker link has its own proof budget without a 10-minute interval. The first-confirmation exemption applies once per typed address (lowercase) and webinar, and never to an address with a `+tag`; the canonical mailbox (lowercase, `+tag` removed, dots removed for gmail, googlemail folded into gmail) keys the per-address total and the per-flow caps only. The stored and used address stay as typed. Assumed derogations: a legitimate `+tag` address can be pre-empted by a third party who fills the `:unproven` total of the mailbox with other `+N` variants, leaving it without a confirmation mail for at most 24 hours; and each dotted form of a gmail address has its own exemption, so N forms give N mails, bounded by the IP limit (10 per minute per IPv4 or per /64; a /48 holds 65,536 /64s). The residual flood per address and day is bounded by the number of webinars open for registration N: 10 resends + N first confirmations + N instant-access mails + 6N speaker links; confirmed registrations receive no additional mail. A mail sent to an address nobody has proven (confirmation, API request, speaker link) never repeats the name typed in the form: the greeting is neutral. No newsletter consent is implied. Once confirmed, every registration is pushed to the CRM as a participant (when `CRM_SYNC_ENABLED=1`); prospection still requires the separate marketing consent. Cross-origin registrations from a partner site and the CRM rules are described in [registrations-api.md](registrations-api.md). The first confirmation of a person sends, once, the link to their personal calendar feed, and a confirmed registration is told by mail when its webinar is moved or cancelled: [agenda.md](agenda.md). The day before the webinar, every confirmed registration receives a reminder with its personal `/r/:token` access link: [registration-reminders.md](registration-reminders.md).

`GET /r/:token` has no effect while the registration is pending: it shows a page with a confirmation button (link scanners follow GET links, and confirming now enrolls the person in the CRM). `POST /r/:token` (the button, CSRF-protected) confirms the registration and stores its ID in an encrypted, HTTP-only, SameSite=Lax session cookie (Secure over HTTPS). For an already confirmed registration, `GET /r/:token` remains the access link: it sets the cookie and redirects, with no CRM effect. `CurrentRegistration` resolves only registrations of `@webinar` with room access: confirmed, or with instant access (below), never removed from the room. Viewer tokens require this confirmed registration on the requested published webinar. Their stable identity is `viewer-<registration_id>` and their display name is the registration name. Two tabs for the same registration have the same LiveKit identity: the second connection replaces the first. Host and signed speaker resolution precede the viewer gate. Live visitors without registration see the registration page without a polling countdown, preventing reload loops.

The Rails POST limiter permits ten requests per minute per client across local Puma workers (IPv4 address, or the /64 prefix of an IPv6 address — `ClientNetwork`), with one counter shared by this form and `POST /api/registrations` (`scope: :registrations`). A file cache on the persistent storage volume uses one stable process lock around increment and periodic cleanup, including counter creation. IPs are hashed; expired counters are purged at most once a minute when requests arrive. This is scoped to a single application host sharing the volume; multiple independent hosts require a shared network store.

## Instant access during the live

While the webinar is live, or within the 15 minutes before its planned start (`Webinar#instant_access_open?`), a registration **created** by the submission opens the room at once on the registering device: `registrations.instant_access_at` is set, the cookie is stored and the browser is redirected to the room. The confirmation mail is still sent. The double opt-in is unchanged: neither `confirmed_at` nor `crm_enrolled_at` is set, so nothing reaches the CRM until the person clicks the mail link. A registration found by its email never gets instant access (typing someone else's address does not enter under their name): it follows the usual pending flow. Outside the window, the room still opens through the mail link only.

### Provisional identity

An identity opened by instant access has no proven mailbox: it stays **provisional** (`Registration#provisional?`) and is never promoted into the confirmed identity of another browser.

- **Identity cookie**: `registration_id` carries the registration id **and** its random `identity_secret` (`RoomRemoval#registration_in_browser`, constant-time compare). A cookie without the right secret designates nobody: another registration, or the former format (id alone). Cookies set before this change therefore no longer give access; the mail link sets them again. The former format is still read by the **removal** checks only (`removed_registration_in_browser`), so a viewer removed before the change stays out.
- **Confirmation from the browser that holds the provisional identity** (id + secret): ordinary confirmation, mailbox and browser both proven; the person keeps their exchanges.
- **Confirmation from another browser**: `GET /r/:token` asks for a first name (no effect); the `POST` turns the provisional identity into a **tombstone** and creates a **new, confirmed registration** for the person who clicked, under the name and spectator / participant choice given on that page, with a new secret and a cookie set by this POST (`Registration#hand_over!`); an **access mail** (`RegistrationMailer#access`) gives its personal `/r/:token` link to open it on another browser. Speakers linked to the provisional registration (always on mailbox proof, `Speaker#registration`) follow to the new one. The tombstone keeps its id, secret, removal and exchanges (chat, hand, `viewer-<id>` tracks) for the browser that opened it, but its address becomes `retire-<32 random hex>@retire.invalid`, its link token changes and its marketing request is cancelled: never confirmable (`Registration#confirm!` refuses a removed or tombstoned registration under the row lock), never pushed to the CRM, never attributed to the mailbox owner. A browser already registered to the webinar, or removed from it, gets 409 / 403 and nothing changes.
- **Legitimate case**: registering on the computer during the live, then confirming on the phone, gives the phone a confirmed registration; the computer keeps watching under the provisional identity, and what was said from the computer is **not** on the confirmed record. The confirmation mail says so: it names the person who entered (« quelqu'un est entré sous le nom X avec votre adresse ») and advises confirming from the same browser to keep the exchanges.
- **Removal of an unconfirmed registration** (régie): `room_removed_at` first, in its own transaction, then the tombstone. The address is freed (nothing proved it belonged to the removed person); the id and `room_removed_at` stay, so the browser trace holds (`RoomRemoval`). A failed tombstone (collision, database) is logged and leaves the address removed, never the room open. A confirmed registration keeps its address, removed with it; so does an unconfirmed registration removed through a speaker linked to it (mailbox proven by `/rattacher`), so that this speaker cannot register again under the same address.
- **Reserved domain**: no typed address may end in `.invalid` (public form, API, private link: `Registration.reserved_email?`); only tombstones carry it, so nobody can occupy a tombstone address in advance.
- Tracks of a provisional identity stay on its LiveKit identity; the episode manifest names them « non vérifié » (`EpisodeManifest#display_name`). `crm:erase` finds tombstones by `retired_email_digest` (HMAC of the freed address).

A `participant` registration enters the room with a raised hand (`HandRaiseControl#raise_declared_hand`, on its first token only — a hand lowered, refused or returned stays down); the régie flags it « veut intervenir en vidéo » and the host admits it with « Donner la parole ». The entry token never publishes: publication follows `PublishPermission` (image consent AND granted hand), opened by the server ([hand-raises.md](hand-raises.md)). Admitted without consent, the consent window opens by itself in the room (`hand_raise_viewer.jsx`, hand `wants_video`, once per hand); only its `POST /api/participant_consent` makes the server open the publication.

## Private participants link (`/i?t=`)

The shared speaker invitation link became the private participants link: name, **mandatory email** and the same choice, `participant` checked by default (`InvitationEntry`). Participant: a `Speaker` who enters the stage at once through their personal `/w/:slug/join?t=` link, and publishes as soon as they give their image consent (`PublishPermission`, no admission). A found registration is never modified. Spectator: the registration alone, with instant access in the same window as above, otherwise the mail link. Every unconfirmed registration receives the confirmation mail. A browser removed from the webinar, or an email whose registration was removed from the room, gets 403 and nothing is created. Existing speakers without email stay valid.

### Proven link only

The speaker is attached to the registration of that email (`speakers.registration_id`) **only when the mailbox is proven**; otherwise anyone could type someone else's address, have their own speech attributed to that contact, and get them removed with them (the moderation cascade). Creating the registration proves nothing: an attacker can type a victim's new address.

| Proof | When |
|---|---|
| Cookie | the registration is **confirmed**, never had instant access, and the browser carries its `registration_id` cookie — hence set by the POST of the mail link |
| Mailbox | the person clicks the button of `/rattacher?t=` (POST), reached from the `speaker_link` mail sent to that address, which names the speaker |

The link of the `speaker_link` mail is valid 14 days (`Speaker::LINK_TTL`, then 410).

Every other case, new address included: the speaker still enters at once, but the address stays **declared, unverified** on the speaker (`speakers.declared_email`, single-use `link_token`), the address receives the `speaker_link` mail (« confirm you spoke under the name X »; plus the usual confirmation mail when the registration is unconfirmed), and nothing is attributed to any record until the click. Confirming the registration links nothing: a victim whose new address was typed by someone else would otherwise confirm a record carrying that person's speech. The click proves the mailbox for this speaker only, never for a **provisional** identity opened under that address by instant access (maybe by someone else): that registration becomes a tombstone without being removed, stays with the browser that opened it, and the speaker is linked to a **new, unconfirmed** registration of the address (confirmation mail, double opt-in intact), which carries the attribution and the moderation cascade from then on. Same when the address only has a tombstone. A speaker removed from the room before the click is no longer linked; removing an unproven speaker never touches the registration of the same email (the speaker cookie keeps that browser out). `Speaker#registration` finds the contact of a speaker, hence of their chat messages and `speaker-<id>` transcript segments, and is the only path the moderation cascade (`ParticipantModeration`) and any CRM attribution follow. The declared address is forgotten by `crm:erase` and by the 12-month retention purge.

## Activation dependency

`REGISTRATION_PRIVACY_URL` must point to an HTTPS privacy document verified by the operator. Without it the form is unavailable and POST returns 503 (the private participants link `/i` too, since it collects an email). No default is supplied: a URL that answers is not proof of a privacy document, so each operator checks its own. No legal terms were invented for this feature. Configure the variable through the deployment environment only after publication and verification of the applicable policy.

When the configured mail transport is `:test`, the pending page explicitly says this is a trial and no email is sent. It never exposes a confirmation token. Browser links in the test suite use a fixture privacy URL; tests never deliver external mail.
